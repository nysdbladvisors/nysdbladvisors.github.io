NEW YORK PAID FAMILY LEAVE GUIDE

What to Do If Your NY Paid Family Leave Claim Is Delayed or Denied

You applied, and now you’re waiting or you got a “no.” Here’s what the carrier owes you, what to do next, and where to turn for help.

The Short Version

Delayed?

In most cases the carrier must pay or deny within 18 calendar days of receiving your complete request or your first day of leave, whichever is later. If it’s taking longer, contact the carrier, keep notes, and know that you can request arbitration over a late payment or a late denial.

Denied?

The carrier must give you the reason and information on requesting arbitration. A neutral arbitrator can review the decision through NAM (National Arbitration and Mediation). The state doesn’t post a deadline, so read your letter and act promptly.

Step 1: Find Out Where Your Claim Stands

Before anything else, pin down the facts. They’ll help whether you’re calling the carrier or preparing for arbitration.

Write down your dates

Note the day your leave started, the day you sent your request to the carrier, and the day the carrier received it if you have a delivery confirmation.

Check the 18-day clock

The 18 days start from the carrier’s receipt of your complete request or your first day of leave, whichever is later. Count from the later date.

Gather what you sent

Pull together your copies of form PFL-1 and any certification, proof or other documents you sent. For family care, that includes form PFL-4.

Contact the carrier

Call or write to ask the status. Keep notes of the date, who you spoke with, and what they said. Follow up in writing when you can.

Step 2: If Your Claim Is Denied

A denial isn’t always the end. Here is what you should receive and what you can do.

Ask for the reason in writing

The carrier (or your employer, if self-insured) must give you the reason for the denial and information about requesting arbitration. Keep the letter. It’s the key document.

Request arbitration

You can ask for a review by a neutral arbitrator through NAM (National Arbitration and Mediation). The state’s Paid Family Leave site points to nyspfla.namadr.com for this.

Don’t wait on the deadline

The state’s Paid Family Leave site doesn’t list a deadline to request arbitration. Check your denial letter for instructions, and act as soon as you can.

Fix what’s fixable

If something was missing or inconsistent, such as a blank employer section or mismatched dates, gather the correct documents. Compare your paperwork to the carrier’s stated reason.

Other disputes qualify too. Arbitration isn’t only for denials. You can also request it for other claim problems, such as a late payment or a late denial.

If Your Employer Retaliates

Your employer can’t discriminate or retaliate against you for requesting or taking Paid Family Leave. Retaliation can include not returning you to the same or a comparable job, firing you, cutting your pay or benefits, or disciplining you.

Step A: Request reinstatement

Complete the Formal Request for Reinstatement (Form PFL-DC-119), file it with your employer, and send a copy to Paid Family Leave, PO Box 9030, Endicott, NY 13761-9030. Your employer has 30 calendar days to respond.

Step B: File a complaint

If you aren’t reinstated, are unhappy with the response, or hear nothing in 30 days, complete the Discrimination/Retaliation Complaint Form (Form PFL-DC-120) with the required documents. A hearing is scheduled within 45 calendar days.

Possible remedies include reinstatement, back wages, attorney’s fees and penalties of up to $500. You may also file with the State Division of Human Rights, the U.S. EEOC, the NYS Department of Labor or a local human rights commission, depending on the situation. Wrong deductions from your paycheck? Raise it with your employer first, then file a complaint with the state.

Common Questions

Quick answers about delayed and denied claims.

How long does the carrier have to decide my Paid Family Leave claim?

In most cases the carrier must pay or deny within 18 calendar days of receiving your complete request or your first day of leave, whichever is later.

What if my claim is denied?

The carrier (or your employer, if self-insured) must give you the reason and information about requesting arbitration. You can request review by a neutral arbitrator through NAM, National Arbitration and Mediation.

Is there a deadline to request arbitration?

The state’s Paid Family Leave site doesn’t state a deadline, so check your denial letter and act promptly. Don’t wait.

Can I request arbitration if my payment is late?

Yes. Arbitration is available for other claim disputes too, such as late payment or a late denial.

What if my employer won’t give me my job back?

You can file a formal request for reinstatement (Form PFL-DC-119) with your employer. If you aren’t reinstated or don’t get a response within 30 calendar days, you can file a discrimination and retaliation complaint (Form PFL-DC-120) with the Workers’ Compensation Board.

Can you represent me in arbitration?

No. We are not a law firm, and our service covers paperwork, guidance and process support. We can help you understand the steps and organize your documents.
