Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about NY Paid Family Leave and disability benefits. Official NYS PFL information ↗

What’s the difference between PFL and DBL?

Paid Family Leave (PFL) provides paid time off to bond with a new child, care for a family member with a serious health condition, or meet certain military-family needs. Disability Benefits (DBL) provides partial wage replacement when you can’t work because of your own non-work-related illness, injury, or pregnancy-related disability.

Can I take Paid Family Leave to care for a sick parent or other family member?

Often, yes. PFL can cover time off to care for a family member with a serious health condition, and covered relatives include a spouse, child, or parent, among others. The official NYS PFL website lists who qualifies, and our free questionnaire review can help you see whether your situation appears to fit.

How much does PFL pay?

PFL generally pays 67% of your average weekly wage for up to 12 weeks, up to a weekly cap. The cap is $1,228.53 for leave starting in 2026 and $1,287.91 for leave starting in 2027. Use the calculator above for an estimate. Your insurance carrier determines the actual benefit.

Do I have to tell my employer before I take leave?

If your need for leave is foreseeable, such as a planned birth or scheduled care, you generally must give your employer at least 30 days’ notice. For unforeseen needs, notify them as soon as possible. Check the official NYS PFL website for current rules.

Do I have to pay to apply?

No. You can apply directly using free official forms and instructions. Our optional paid assistance is available if you want hands-on help with paperwork and submission. Consultations are always free, with no obligation.

Are you part of New York State or an insurance company?

No. We are an independent advisory business and are not affiliated with New York State, the Workers’ Compensation Board, or any insurance carrier. Eligibility, approval, and benefit amounts are determined by the carrier or claims administrator.

I’m due in late 2026. Will I get the 2026 or 2027 rate?

Your benefit rate is generally based on the first day of your leave: leave that starts in 2026 uses the 2026 rate, and leave that starts on or after January 1, 2027 uses the 2027 rate. If you’re bonding with a new child, you can generally take and complete your leave at any time within the first 12 months after birth, adoption, or foster placement, as long as you remain an eligible employee.
